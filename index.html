Prologue · The art of weaving

Before the saree, the thread

Knowing how a saree is made helps you choose the right one. Scroll slowly and watch a handloom saree come to life, one thread at a time.

Fig. I · The thread

Chapter I

It begins with a single thread

Long before a saree is draped, it is thousands of threads. Silk is reeled from cocoons, twisted, dyed and wound, then stretched lengthwise on the loom.

A six-yard saree can hold several thousand warp threads.

Chapter II

Warp and weft

The warp runs the length of the saree. The weaver throws a shuttle across it, row after row, passing the weft over and under. The rhythm of the loom goes on for days, sometimes weeks.

Over, under. Over, under. That is the whole secret, repeated by hand.

Chapter III

Ikat: the pattern is dyed first

In ikat, bundles of thread are tied and dyed before weaving. When they meet on the loom, the colours slide into diamonds, birds and elephants, with a soft feathered edge only the hand can make.

Patola dyes both warp and weft, so every motif has to align twice.

Chapter IV

Brocade and zari

Extra threads of zari, silk wrapped in fine metal, are woven in to raise motifs from the cloth: buttas, temple borders, peacocks. This is what makes a Kanchi or a Banarasi glow.

Watch the buttas catch the light.

Chapter V

The interlock

Some weaves join two colours by hand, thread by thread. Kanchipuram's korvai border and the Paithani pallu are interlocked so tightly that the join becomes part of the weave itself.

Two colours, woven to never part.

Chapter VI

Tissue: woven light

When zari runs through the weft itself, the whole cloth shines. A tissue silk changes colour with every step you take: gold, copper, rose.

Light, crisp, and impossible to ignore.
